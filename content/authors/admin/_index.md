I am a sociologist using quantitative and computational methods and legal analysis to study collective intelligence and the social impact of AI. I am currently a Postgraduate Research Associate in Princeton University’s Department of Sociology, where I completed my doctoral training in Sociology and Statistics & Machine Learning.

My research investigates the transformation of financial markets and economic actors after the **digital turn**, and the social, technical, and regulatory implications of **multi-agent AI systems**. I also work on the **responsible use of AI** for decision-making, social science research, and teaching.

One of the key questions of our time is how an information ecosystem characterized by massive amounts of linked digital data and the large-scale deployment of predictive, generative, and agentic AI systems will reshape markets and social institutions.

To address this question, my postdoctoral projects examine **how unequal distributions of information and computational capacity change collective decision-making, market dynamics, and the rules needed to govern institutions**. I approach this problem from two complementary directions: by modeling collective computation under unequal access to information, and by examining how law should adapt to AI-driven changes in the scale of social activity.
